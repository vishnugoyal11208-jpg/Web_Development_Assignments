Science Notes

Physics

Einstein's Mass-Energy Equivalence :

Mass of any object is equal to the product of its energy and the square of the speed of light.
E = mc2
where E is energy, m is mass, and c is the speed of light.

Newton's Second Law :

The acceleration of an object is directly proportional to the net force acting on it and inversely proportional to its mass.
F = ma
Where F is force, m is mass, and a is acceleration.

Universal Law of Gravitation :

Every particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.
F = G(m₁m₂)/r2
Where F is the gravitational force, G is the gravitational constant, m1 and m2 are the masses of the two objects, and r is the distance between their centers.

Chemistry

Avogadro's Number :

Avogadro's number is the number of constituent particles (usually atoms or molecules) in one mole of a substance.


NA = 6.022 x 1023 mol-1
where NA is Avogadro's number.

Ideal Gas Law :

The ideal gas law describes the relationship between pressure, volume, temperature, and the number of moles of an ideal gas.
PV = nRT
where P is pressure, V is volume, n is the number of moles, R is the ideal gas constant, and T is temperature.
